## Try using the Pomodoro Method to boost your concentration.

Working for periods can decrease your concentration and productivity levels, in the brain according to studies and researches conducted on this subject matter over time. To combat this issue effectively and improve work efficiency and performance significantly over time a technique known as the Pomodoro Technique was introduced by Francesco Cirillo; it involves breaking down work sessions into 25 minute intervals with 5 minute breaks in, between each interval to enhance focus and productivity levels. 

Short bursts of work can help fend off exhaustion by giving your mind a chance to recharge frequently and sustain focus. 
**Scheduled Rest Periods**; Taking breaks revitalizes the mind. Enhances both decision making skills and overall task involvement. 

I have a friend who writes and used to have trouble, with putting off work even though he spent hours at his desk every day. By using technique intervals to break up his work into bursts of focused activity instead of long stretches of time procrastinating like before he found that he was able to be more productive and create his best work without feeling exhausted. 

## Use Time Blocking, to Safeguard Your Daily Agenda

Time blocking turns intentions into schedules according to productivity expert Cal Newport who stated that a 40 hour workweek organized with time blocks can yield as much output, as a disorganized 60 hour workweek. 

"Established Schedule Blocks"; Designate time slots, for activities to transform unclear goals into tangible achievements more efficiently. 
Avoiding multitasking is key, to maintaining clarity in your tasks as it helps establish boundaries, between priorities. 

A friend who runs her business always extended her office hours. Ended up feeling swamped with work tasks. She established time blocks, for tasks. Dedicating mornings to creative client projects and afternoons to handling emails and administrative tasks. As a result of this approach she now experiences defined weeks where each hour spent working serves a clear purpose. 

## Minimize Changing Tasks Frequently and Shifting Contexts. 

Switching between tasks can make you feel like you have work to do than you actually do – something psychologist Gerald Weinberg pointed out when he mentioned that moving back and forth between projects could lead to an 80 percent drop, in productivity, per shift. 

**Focus, on One Task, at a Time**; Make a decision to concentrate on completing one task before moving on to the next one to enhance your focus and productivity. 
Grouping tasks together can help you maintain your focus and productivity throughout the day by tackling activities, like emails and calls in batches, than jumping between them sporadically. 

One graphic designer I mentored discovered that she was constantly switching between client projects and working into the evenings without much progress being made on any of them. However for her productivity, to. Reclaim the lost time consumed by transitioning contexts she learned to group design tasks into categories. 

## Strategically Outsource Non Essential Tasks

It's not feasible or advisable to manage every task on your own according to research that shows CEOs who delegate successfully achieve outcomes without extending work hours unnecessarily. 

When evaluating your core competencies focus on tasks that align best with your skills and experience and consider delegating or outsourcing responsibilities. 
Regularly review your belongings to determine what you no longer need and identify chances to delegate tasks that have been overlooked. 

I once had a manager who shared that he used to triple check reports which made his workweek longer, than necessary. However after realizing that his real strengths lied in thinking than, in proofreading he decided to delegate these non core tasks to others. As a result his working hours significantly decreased while his performance improved considerably.
