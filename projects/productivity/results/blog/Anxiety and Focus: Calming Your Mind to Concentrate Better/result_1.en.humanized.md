## Practice Breathing, to Focus Your Mind 

When feeling anxious and needing to calm down quickly­­­—a technique is to practice breathing instead of regular breathing­­­—which involves paying close attention to your breath as you inhale and exhale deeply to help ease stress right away­­—by triggering your bodys relaxation response and moving you from a state of heightened alertness, to a more relaxed state. 

One easy method to begin with is the **technique of 4–7–8 breathing**, which was made famous by Dr Andrew Weils association with it. To start with this technique; Inhale deeply, for four seconds; hold your breath steadily for seven seconds;. Then exhale gradually over the course of eight seconds. Dr Weil claims that this method is a " agent, for the nervous system" and suggests that regular practice can visibly reduce feelings of anxiety.

Many people misunderstand mindfulness as requiring stretches of doing nothing when in reality incorporating short but intentional mindfulness breaks, throughout your day can significantly lessen anxiety driven distractions. These quick "micro mindfulness breaks," which involve pausing tasks for a couple of minutes to actively tune into your senses and regain focus. 

A used technique is the **Five Senses Exercise**, in which you take a moment to recognize five things you can see around you. Four things you can touch or feel. Three sounds you hear. Two scents you smell. And one taste or focus on your breathing pattern or sensation in that moment. Dr.Jon Kabat Zinn describes mindfulness as "being fully attentive, in a manner; focusing on the now without judgment " gently redirect your attention back, to the moment.

## Enhance Your Focus by Restricting Multitasking.

Multitasking may seem like an idea, for getting more done quickly. Research consistently shows that it's not as effective as it seems to be, at first glance. Studies indicate that multitasking can raise anxiety levels by overloading the brain and making it harder to concentrate effectively. Focusing on one task at a time—referred to as tasking—can actually help relax your mind and enhance your ability to concentrate sharply. 

Blocking out time periods, for tasks can be really beneficial, in boosting productivity and reducing stress levels according to Stanford neuroscientist Dr.Clifford Nass who strongly recommends this approach.His assertion that multitasking not diminishes performance but also leads to anxiety by overwhelming the brain underscores the importance of giving attention to tasks as a way to alleviate anxiety.
